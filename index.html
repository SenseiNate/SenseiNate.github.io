How I got here.

Ten years in three parts.

Then

Seven years in the Air Force as a Munitions Systems Engineer. The job was easy to describe and hard to do: know where every asset is, all the time, and never be wrong about it. That is where I learned to build systems instead of trusting people to remember, and where I first led a team.

Since

Northrop Grumman taught me formal systems engineering: model-based architecture, requirements traceability, and chairing governance boards. GE Healthcare added human factors engineering and failure modes and risk analysis, designing and validating features with the clinicians who would actually use them.

Now

At BAE Systems I own a $60M+ integration program on the customer side, govern technical interfaces across five partner organizations, and lead independent review on a contested integration to protect program and financial interests before acceptance.
